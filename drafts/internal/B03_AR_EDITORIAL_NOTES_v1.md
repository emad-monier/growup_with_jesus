## Claim and safety checks

- Fictional opening scene. No real minor is represented and it is not presented as a true story.
- No claim that anger, sadness, or loneliness is sin, defect, weak faith, or a disorder.
- No individual diagnosis and no medical rule derived from a Bible verse.
- No general ruling on Communion, fasting, or local Church practice.
- Faith is never presented as a substitute for a clinician, counsellor, or safeguarding action.
- Escalation language covers harm to self, harm to others, persistent distress, and withdrawal,
  and points to a trusted adult rather than to self-diagnosis. Local emergency routes must be
  adapted by a responsible adult before publication.
- Quoted Scripture is short; the two chapter-mapped verses carry the thread, and the
  narrative quotations (مر 14، يو 11، رو 12) are brief and kept in their context.
- Both Christ-life scenes use only the biblical text and the named Coptic Encyclopedia
  commentary; no dialogue, feeling, or motive is invented, and each reference is named in
  the reader copy.
- Each application is introduced with «قد يضيء لنا هذا الموقف…» and tied to a specific
  commentary; it is not presented as a proof that the story solves every question.
- Tears and grief are not called defeat or weak faith; sitting with a grieving person is
  called a quiet service, not a substitute for safeguarding or clinical help.
- Pastoral and commentary material is paraphrased and attributed; no hosted text is quoted.
- Direct Scripture quotation remains subject to edition/permissions confirmation for any
  published edition.
